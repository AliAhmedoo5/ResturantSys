# Manager reporting gaps and documentation audit

Reviewed 29 October 2026. Scope: the request to identify missing/partial reporting and see which calendar dates, Business Days and times have the most orders and money movement. Documentation and source inspection establish the findings below; live restaurant data was not queried.

Expanded review: all 13 Manager report families, report navigation, filters, comparisons, source inspection and exports. This inventory distinguishes missing reporting, stored evidence that is not projected, incomplete historical evidence and stale explanations. Acceptance criteria below describe work still required; they are not claims that those changes have been implemented. Findings are based on source inspection rather than new runtime tests.

## User decisions confirmed during review

- **Primary reporting basis: Business Day.** Keep the assigned Business Day as the main operational grouping, including overnight trading. Also expose actual calendar dates and times for dashboard analytics; do not treat calendar date and Business Day as interchangeable.
- **Visible time zone: Pakistan time (Asia/Karachi).** The user's answer establishes display/reporting time. It does not require replacing the master policy's UTC audit storage; retain UTC/offset evidence internally and display the corresponding Pakistan time. Legacy uncertain timestamps retain their coverage caveats.
- **Payroll reporting cadence and attribution: monthly earned period.** Show October salary in October's monthly payroll even if it is recorded or paid in November. Show the actual November payment in November's money-flow analytics, preserve the immutable recording date, and do not rewrite original closed reports. Detailed allocation formulas and correction mechanics still require specification before implementation.
- **Money-flow coverage: all sources, separated.** Include physical drawer cash, bank/digital movements and owner funds as distinct funding streams. Bank/digital and owner entries must not change physical drawer balances; linked events must not be counted twice.
- **Peak-order activity: all confirmed orders.** Count each confirmed order once, including orders subsequently cancelled or refunded, and show cancellations/refunds separately. Keep workload counts separate from valid sales value and actual money collected; revisions do not create new orders.
- **Late-entered payments: retain both times.** Support an actual payment/event date and time alongside the immutable recording timestamp. When entered promptly, the actual time can default to the entry time; a late entry can supply the actual time explicitly. Historical unknown event times must remain unknown. This decision does not authorize rewriting a closed Business Day or its sealed report.
- **Separate order and collection Shift attribution.** An order created on Cashier A's Shift remains attributed to that originating Shift. A later collection received on Cashier B's Shift belongs to the receiving Shift's money-flow report. Retain a link between the order and collection and expose both relationships; recording/approval actors are distinct fields.
- **Fair weekday comparisons: totals and completed-day averages.** Show both aggregate order totals and average orders per completed Business Day for each weekday, with the completed-day denominator visible. Use the average as the primary weekday comparison so unequal numbers of weekdays in a selected period do not bias the ranking. Current incomplete Business Days remain separately labeled.
- **Closed-day corrections: append separately.** Preserve original closed Business Day records and sealed reports. A missed historical payment is represented by a linked correction with its actual event time, affected Business Day and recording time. Clearly distinguish the original report from a correction-adjusted view. Do not create a new physical drawer movement today for cash that already moved on the earlier day; reconcile its existing evidence and prevent double counting. Correction authority, storage and reconciliation mechanics must be specified before implementation.
- **Historical names: original and current, distinguished.** Retain transaction-time names for staff, suppliers and categories, with their current names available alongside where useful. Preserve stable IDs and existing snapshots; label unavailable older name history rather than inventing snapshots from today's names.
- **Confirmed first-phase priority.** Implement accurate Shift money-flow reporting, calendar-date/Business Day/time views and busiest-period analytics first. Subsequent phases cover the other reporting gaps. This establishes delivery priority; the acceptance criteria and implementation work remain outstanding.

These are reporting requirements confirmed in conversation, not implemented changes. Business choices for the first reporting phase are captured above. Detailed correction/allocation mechanics and later-phase financial policies still require an implementation specification; unknown historical evidence must not be fabricated.

## Additions needed to specify this request precisely

1. A selectable time basis: Business Day for operations; actual local calendar date/time for event activity. Display both on each source row, plus Cashier, Shift, source ID and funding source. Show order time separately from actual collection/refund/handover time and financial posting time. Example: 30 October at 01:00 can belong to calendar date 30 October and the Business Day opened on 29 October.
2. Order peaks: order count, order value and average ticket by distinct Business Day ID/opening date, local calendar date, weekday and hour; ranked busiest periods; optional weekday × hour table/heatmap. Define eligible statuses, zero-activity hours, ties and overnight weekday basis. Weekday averages need completed-day denominators so a range with five Fridays and four Mondays is not misread. “Most orders” and “highest value” must be separate rankings.
3. Money peaks: inflow, outflow and net flow as separate series by actual event timestamp, Cashier/Shift and funding source. Define drawer cash separately from recorded bank/owner movements. Exclude salary accruals and unpaid supplier credit from physical flows. Prevent duplicate receipt/settlement/movement counting.
4. One selected-Shift reconciliation: opening float, customer receipts, rider handovers, other pay-ins, refunds, expenses, supplier payments, payroll/advances, cash drops, expected cash, blind count and signed variance. Related rows, totals and exports must share the same meaningful scope.
5. Receipt-event attribution and coverage: independently recorded receipt/refund/settlement timestamp, receiving actor/Shift/Business Day and original order link. Do not invent historical collection times. Show coverage/missing-history notices and an explicit legacy order-cohort mode where only that evidence exists.
6. Reconcile the older manager guide/spec with newer finance/costing features. Describe transaction-time versus Business Day filters, peak formulas and export metadata in the user guide and executable acceptance specification.

No app changes, framework commands or CI runs were performed for this audit. No numerical claim about the restaurant's actual busiest periods is possible without its data.
